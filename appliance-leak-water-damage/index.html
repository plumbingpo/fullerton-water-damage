Appliance Leak Water Damage Help in Fullerton

Three appliances cause most of the leaks we see. Washer supply hoses can burst and keep spraying at full pressure until the valve is closed. Dishwashers usually leak a little at a time, at the drain hose or door seal, hidden beneath the cabinets. Behind a refrigerator, the thin tube feeding the ice maker may drip for months. Cleanup is different for each of them.

Our Fullerton crew starts by pulling the appliance out and finding where the water went. Kitchen leaks tend to end up beneath the cabinets and underneath the floor. With a laundry leak we look at the rooms next to it and the wall behind the washer, plus the ceiling below when the laundry sits on an upper floor. Then our team removes the standing water, meters the materials that soaked it up, and places equipment matched to the size of the damage.

Do This First

Your First Steps

  1. Turn Off the ApplianceCancel the cycle, and pull the plug only when the floor and the outlet near it are dry. If not, switch it off at the breaker instead.
  2. Shut Off the Water LineFor a washing machine, close both the cold and hot valves on the wall at its back. The dishwasher valve is under the kitchen sink. The small valve for an ice maker line sits behind the refrigerator or underneath.
  3. Soak Up Standing WaterGetting reachable water up with a mop or towels stops it from moving under the cabinets and the floor. Vacuums are safe for this only when built for wet and dry pickup.
  4. Leave the Door ShutA dishwasher or washer that still holds water should stay closed. When the door opens mid-cycle, the full load of water can pour out on the floor.
  5. Document What FailedKeep the part that failed, whether a hose, a fitting or a valve, and get photos of it. Both the appliance maker and your insurer may want to look at it.
Will Insurance Pay?

Does Insurance Cover It?

Homeowners policies often cover sudden and accidental events, and a burst hose or a machine overflowing with no warning is that kind of event. The policy typically pays for the water damage, not for the appliance or the failed part. Slow seepage from a dishwasher or ice maker line is a harder claim, because gradual leaks usually are not covered. Certain insurers will want to know the hose's age and whether it was kept up. Before cleanup, take photos, save the failed part and read your policy.

Common Causes

What Causes It

  • A failed water inlet valve that keeps filling the washer or dishwasher after it should stop.
  • Installation mistakes like a loose supply line connection or a dishwasher that does not sit level.
  • Plastic or copper ice maker lines that kink, crack or loosen at the fitting.
  • Too much detergent or an overloaded drum that makes the machine overflow.
  • Worn dishwasher door gaskets, cracked drain hoses or a failed pump seal.
Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

Mold Behind Appliances

The warm, dark space behind a refrigerator or dishwasher rarely gets checked. Long before it is discovered, a slow drip back there can be growing mold across the floor and wall.

Kitchen Cabinets

Toe kicks and cabinet sides take on water from underneath when a dishwasher leaks. Swelling and separation follow, and for weeks the soaked cabinet boxes may trap moisture against the wall.

Ceiling Below an Upstairs Laundry

The ceiling under an upper floor laundry catches whatever water makes it past the floor above. Sagging, staining and soaked insulation above the drywall are the usual result.

Flooring and Subfloor

Water slides beneath laminate, vinyl plank and hardwood. Boards cup or swell, and the subfloor underneath stays wet long after the surface looks dry.
