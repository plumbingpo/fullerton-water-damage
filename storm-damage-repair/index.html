Why Hire a Professional for Storm Damage Repair?

Because one kind of storm damage feeds another, the work needs restoration professionals with real experience. A roof leak that looks minor from inside may have done extensive hidden damage to insulation, roof decking and wall cavities, and that leads to mold growth and structural deterioration if it is not found and dried. With moisture meters and thermal cameras, trained technicians follow the route water took and find all the areas it reached.

Tarping and boarding up only work when they are done with the right technique. If a tarp is fastened poorly, it can direct water where you do not want it or give out when the wind picks up. Experienced in storm mitigation, our team picks appropriate methods and materials to keep the building protected until permanent repair work can be scheduled.

How to Tell You Need Storm Damage Repair

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Attic or wall insulation that is damp or matted down following a storm
  • Damp, earthy odors that appear a few days after the storm
  • Downspouts knocked off and gutter runs hanging after high wind
  • Missing, damaged or lifted shingles you can see from the ground
  • A basement or ground floor that floods when the drains cannot keep up
  • Water pooling in the attic, around the chimney or near skylights
  • Swollen ceiling panels or blistered, peeling paint in the days after a storm

Storm Damage Repair Prevention Tips

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Keep gutters and downspouts clear so water drains properly in heavy rain
  • Good attic insulation and ventilation stop ice dams from forming along the roof edge
  • Make sure the ground around the house slopes away and downspouts discharge well clear of it
  • Reseal worn caulk and weatherstripping around windows and doors before the rainy season
  • Learn where the main water shutoff is and how to close it if pipes are damaged in a storm
  • In areas that see hurricanes, fit storm shutters or windows rated for impact
