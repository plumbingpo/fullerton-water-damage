Wall and Drywall Water Damage in Fullerton

A sheet of drywall behaves much as a wick does. When water reaches the bottom of a wall, the gypsum and paper draw it upward, often a foot or more above the level the water ever reached on the floor. The painted face can look and feel normal while the back side and the insulation behind it are soaked. For that reason our team relies on moisture meter readings rather than appearance.

Not every wet wall has to be torn out. When the water was clean and the drywall remains firm, drying it where it stands is often possible, at times by pulling the baseboard and pushing air into the cavity through small holes. If the drywall has gone soft, crumbles, shows mold or took in dirty water, we do a flood cut, meaning a level cut over the damaged area with all material beneath it removed.

Signs to Look For

  • Drywall that feels soft, spongy or crumbles when pressed
  • Door casing and trim joints separating from the drywall
  • Cool, slightly clammy drywall next to walls at normal temperature
  • Flooring along the wall that is damp, stained or starting to lift
  • Bubbling, wrinkled or peeling paint and wallpaper low on the wall
  • Nail or screw heads popping out and seams showing through the paint
Why Speed Matters

The Damage Timeline

The first few hours

Water Starts Climbing

Water at the base of the wall soaks into the drywall edge and starts to climb. Baseboards and the bottom plate take on moisture.

24 to 48 hours

Swelling and Blisters

Moisture keeps climbing, the baseboards puff up and blisters form in the paint. Growth is able to get going on the paper that lines the hidden side.

Days 3 through 7

Panels Lose Strength

The gypsum core turns soft and its paper starts to separate. A musty odor grows stronger while the batts settle and mold covers more of the unseen face.

After a week

Framing Is Affected

By this point the studs and plates have been damp long enough for mold and early rot. Much more wall now has to be removed and rebuilt.

How We Fix It

Our Wall and Drywall Water Damage Process

1

Find Every Wet Area

We scan each wall with a moisture meter and thermal camera and mark how high and how far the water traveled, including the opposite side and adjoining rooms.

2

Decide What Stays

Based on the water category, how long it sat and how the drywall feels, we tell you plainly which sections can be dried in place and which need to come out.

3

Open Up or Air Out

Where the drywall stays, the trim is pulled and airflow is sent inside the wall. Walls that cannot be kept get a flood cut along a level line, with the wet insulation pulled.

4

Drying and Reconstruction

Air movers and dehumidifiers run until studs and remaining drywall read dry, typically 3 to 5 days. The rebuild follows with fresh insulation, drywall installation and finishing, trim and a coat of paint.

Behind the Surface

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Insulation Holding Water

Fiberglass batts soak up water and hold it against the studs. No amount of airflow on the painted side will dry a cavity packed with wet batts.

Moisture in Exterior Walls

On an exterior wall, water can soak the sheathing behind the insulation. Because it is slow to dry, we test it before any new drywall goes up.

Growth Behind the Paint

Paper lining, darkness and stagnant air make the hidden side of a wall a perfect place for mold. It can cover the hidden side while the painted face looks clean.

Electrical Hazards Low Down

Water pools at the same height as most receptacles and cable runs. Until that circuit is inspected, avoid plugging anything into the affected wall.
